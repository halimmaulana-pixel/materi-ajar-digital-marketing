B. Channel Tactics (Sessions 7–10)

Session 7 — Short-Form Video & Creator Content

Key Term
Short-Form Video
Vertical video content typically under 60–90 seconds (Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts) optimized for mobile, sound-on, and fast scrolling. Success depends on a strong hook in the first 1–2 seconds, sustained retention (keeping viewers watching), clear value or entertainment, and captions for sound-off viewing. Lightweight production (phone, natural light, simple editing) can outperform polished ads when it feels authentic.
ElementDefinitionPractical Tip
HookThe opening moment that stops the scroll.Lead with outcome, surprise, or a clear question.
RetentionShare of viewers who keep watching over time.Tight pacing, pattern breaks, on-screen text cues.
UGC StyleContent that feels creator-made rather than corporate.Show real use, honest pros/cons, relatable context.

Session 8 — Email, Automation, CRM & Messaging

Key Term
CRM (Customer Relationship Management)
A system and practice for managing interactions with current and potential customers across the lifecycle. A CRM stores contact data, interaction history, and pipeline stages, enabling segmentation, personalized communication, and sales/service coordination. In marketing, CRM data powers targeted email, messaging, and retention programs.
Key Term
Marketing Automation
Software-driven workflows that trigger messages or actions based on rules and behaviors (e.g., welcome series on signup, cart-abandonment reminder, re-engagement after inactivity). Automation scales timely, relevant communication while reducing repetitive manual work—provided data is clean and consent is respected.

Session 9 — E-commerce, Marketplace & CRO

Key Term
CRO (Conversion Rate Optimization)
A systematic process of increasing the percentage of visitors who complete a desired action (purchase, sign-up) by improving UX, messaging, trust signals, and checkout flow. CRO relies on hypotheses and experiments (often A/B tests) rather than opinion: change one meaningful variable, measure impact, and keep winners.
Key Term
A/B Testing
An experiment comparing two variants (A = control, B = change) shown to similar audiences to measure which performs better on a defined metric (e.g., conversion rate). Valid tests require sufficient sample size, a single primary metric, and controlled conditions to avoid misleading conclusions.

Session 10 — Influencer, Affiliate & Creator Commerce

Key Term
Influencer Tiers & Affiliate
Influencers are often grouped by follower count and engagement: nano/micro (smaller, niche, often higher trust), mid, and macro/mega (large reach). Affiliate marketing pays partners (creators/publishers) a commission per tracked action (sale/lead) via unique links or codes. Transparency matters: paid partnerships and material connections must be clearly disclosed per platform and consumer-protection rules.

C. Measurement & MarTech (Sessions 11–12)

Session 11 — Analytics, Attribution & Performance

Key Term
GA4 (Google Analytics 4)
Google's event-based analytics platform for measuring user interactions across websites and apps. Unlike older session-based models, GA4 treats key actions (page views, clicks, purchases) as events, supports cross-device measurement, and integrates with Google's advertising ecosystem—while requiring careful configuration of events, conversions, and consent.
Key Term
UTM Parameters
Tags appended to a URL (e.g., utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign) that identify where traffic came from. Consistent UTM naming lets analytics tools attribute visits and conversions to specific campaigns, channels, and creatives accurately.
Key Term
Attribution Model
A rule or algorithm that assigns credit for a conversion to touchpoints in the customer journey (e.g., last click, first click, linear, data-driven). Different models tell different stories; choosing one affects budget decisions, so marketers should understand the model's bias and, where possible, complement it with incrementality or lift tests.
MetricFormula / MeaningUse
LTV (Lifetime Value)Expected net revenue from a customer over the relationship.Sets sustainable acquisition spend; compare against CAC.
CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)Total acquisition spend ÷ number of customers acquired.Efficiency of growth; aim for healthy LTV:CAC ratio.
ROASRevenue from ads ÷ ad spend.Ad profitability signal (mind margins and attribution).
Brand Lift / IncrementalityMeasured increase caused by marketing beyond baseline.Tests true causal impact, not just correlation.

Session 12 — MarTech Stack & Data Foundation

Key Term
MarTech Stack
The collection of marketing technologies a company uses (e.g., CMS, CRM, CDP, email/automation, analytics, ad platforms). A good stack is integrated (data flows via APIs/connectors), governed (clear ownership and access), and fit-for-purpose—more tools do not automatically mean better marketing.
Key Term
CDP (Customer Data Platform)
A system that unifies customer data from multiple sources into persistent, individual profiles and makes them available to other tools for segmentation and activation. Unlike a CRM (often relationship/sales-focused) or a DMP (historically cookie/segment-focused), a CDP emphasizes first-party, identifiable data unified for marketing use—always subject to consent and privacy rules.

Key Term
Performance Max / Advantage+ (AI-Driven Campaign Types)
Advertising campaign types (Google and Meta respectively) that use machine learning to automate bidding, targeting, placements, and creative combinations across inventory to meet a specified goal (e.g., conversions). They can improve efficiency but reduce granular manual control, making clear objectives, quality inputs (creative, signals, conversion tracking), and ongoing monitoring essential.
Key Term
AI Agent (in Marketing)
A system that can pursue a goal by taking multi-step actions with some autonomy—such as qualifying leads, answering questions, or orchestrating workflows—often by combining an LLM with tools and data. Unlike a simple rule-based chatbot, an agent can plan and adapt within defined guardrails; it still requires boundaries, logging, and human escalation paths.

Key Term
UU PDP No. 27/2022 (Personal Data Protection Law)
Indonesia's comprehensive personal data protection law. It sets principles for processing personal data (lawfulness, purpose limitation, minimization, accuracy, security, accountability) and grants data subjects rights (access, correction, deletion, etc.). Marketers must ensure they have a valid basis for processing (such as consent where required), provide transparent notices, protect data, and be prepared to respond to rights requests. Non-compliance carries administrative and other sanctions.

E. Case Study: Creator Commerce with Compliance

An electronics store partnered with micro-creators on TikTok Shop Affiliate to promote laptops. Each creator used tracked affiliate links and clearly disclosed paid partnerships. The store monitored sales attribution per creator and protected buyer data in line with privacy obligations. Within one month, sales rose ~40% at lower promotional cost than conventional ads—showing how creator trust, measurable tracking, and compliance can work together.

J. Real-World Case Study & Detailed Solution

Case: Sephora — Omnichannel Personalization with Loyalty, Data & Responsible AI (Reported Real Case)

Background (real, reported problem). Sephora operates in beauty retail where customers expect personalized advice, seamless experiences across app/web/store, and trust in product recommendations. Challenges included unifying customer data across touchpoints, making recommendations relevant at scale, and measuring what truly drives loyalty and sales in a competitive, promotion-heavy market. Sephora's Beauty Insider loyalty program, its app/website experiences, and investments in data-driven personalization and virtual tools (e.g., try-on and tailored recommendations, evolving with AI) are widely reported examples of omnichannel, customer-centric marketing.

The problem statement. "How can a beauty retailer deliver relevant, trusted experiences across channels, increase retention and lifetime value, and optimize marketing spend—while handling customer data responsibly?"

Step-by-Step Reasoning

  1. Unify the customer view: connect touchpoints into coherent profiles so messaging and offers reflect real behavior and preferences (with consent).
  2. Define success by value, not clicks: prioritize LTV, retention, and incremental impact over vanity metrics; measure by stage (RACE) and by cohort.
  3. Personalize with guardrails: use AI to recommend and time interactions, but monitor relevance, avoid bias, and keep humans accountable for brand and fairness.
  4. Close the loop: feed outcomes back into segmentation, creative, and budget allocation—continuous optimization across the lifecycle.
  5. Protect trust: comply with data-protection obligations (purpose, consent, rights, security) and be transparent; in beauty/health-adjacent advice, accuracy and safety matter (manage hallucination/misclaim risk in AI-assisted content).
